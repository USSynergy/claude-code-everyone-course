# Customer Feedback Synthesis
*January – April, 178 total survey responses*

## The Headline

**"Fine but forgettable."**

This exact phrase appeared in 3 separate months of feedback from different customers. It's not a coincidence — it's the diagnosis.

People don't hate Basecamp Rewards. They just don't think about it.

---

## Key Themes

### 1. No Identity, No Personality
The program feels generic — "could be any coffee chain." Customers love Basecamp Coffee but feel the rewards program doesn't match.

> "Your coffee has personality. Your rewards program doesn't. Fix that and you'd have something."

> "The program doesn't feel like Basecamp. It feels like corporate stuff."

### 2. Forgettability Is the Core Problem
Customers forget they're members. They forget they have the app. The program creates no habits.

> "Honestly forgot I was a member until you emailed me this survey."

> "I have the app somewhere on my phone."

- Average app session: 23 seconds
- Most common action: checking point balance (then leaving)

### 3. Tiers Mean Nothing
Nobody understands or cares about Trailblazer, Explorer, or Summit status.

> "I've been coming here for a year and I'm still a Trailblazer. What does that even mean?"

> "What's an Explorer? Am I supposed to care?"

Baristas can't explain the tiers either.

### 4. Customers Want to Feel Known
People want personalization — help discovering drinks, recognition as regulars. The app treats 2-year regulars like strangers.

> "I've been coming here 2 years but the app treats me like a stranger."

> "People light up when we ask about their coffee preferences but the app doesn't capture any of that."

> "I wish you'd help me find new drinks to try. I always get the same thing."

### 5. Promotions Create Temporary Bumps, Nothing More
Valentine's Day double points: +23% app scans that day. Back to baseline the next day. No sustained behavior change.

---

## Competitive Picture

| Competitor | What They Do Well |
|------------|-------------------|
| Starbucks | Games, challenges, personalization |
| Dutch Bros | Relationships — they remember your name |
| Basecamp | Great coffee, warm vibe — but rewards don't reflect this |

NPS for Basecamp overall: **67** (strong)
NPS for Basecamp Rewards: **12** (weak)

That gap is the opportunity.

---

## The Real Problem

Customers have a strong emotional connection to Basecamp Coffee. The rewards program has zero emotional connection. It's a transaction tracker, not a community builder.

The program needs a personality transplant — one that matches the brand customers already love.
